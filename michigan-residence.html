Michigan Residence

Set within a clearing in a mature maple-beech forest, this Michigan residence is organized around a central courtyard shared by a collection of connected buildings. The landscape draws on a French Country vocabulary of gravel, stone, clipped forms, flowering borders, and a traditional fountain, creating a composed garden core with direct visual and physical connections to the surrounding woodland.
Rather than treating the courtyard as a formal set piece, the design balances structure with looseness. Strong axial relationships and defined edges are softened by layered planting, shifting textures, and informal garden rooms, allowing the landscape to move gradually from cultivated enclosure to the wooded perimeter.
Location: Lakeside, MI
Collaborators: John Banks Architects
Status: Completed 2017
